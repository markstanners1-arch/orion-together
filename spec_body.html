Data submission spec

The Orion project pools data from many small scopes into one deep image. Every contributor sends raw frames; one person stacks and processes the lot. The more hours of usable data we collect, the deeper the final image.

This sheet says exactly what to send so your frames can go straight into the stack. Frames that don't follow it may have to be left out, not because the data is bad but because it can't be registered or calibrated against everyone else's.

Target and framing

M42, the Orion Nebula

Centre on the Trapezium (RA 05h 35m 17s, Dec −05° 23′ 28″, J2000). Keep the Running Man (NGC 1977) in the field if your scope can; it will be in most Dwarf and Seestar frames anyway.

  • Centring matters more than rotation. The stack is registered on stars, so any orientation works, but a frame centred a degree off loses most of its overlap.
  • Don't crop, mosaic or pan. One fixed field per session.
  • Don't rotate the field mid-session to "tidy" the framing. Leave it where the goto puts it.
  • If your app offers a framing or mosaic mode, turn it off.

What to capture

Short subs, lots of them

M42's core saturates fast; we recover the Trapezium from the short frames and the faint outer nebula from the sheer number of subs.

SettingWhat to use
Sub length10 s to 30 s (smart scopes: whatever your app offers in that range)
FilterBroadband (no filter, UV/IR cut) for most sessions. If you have a dual-band filter, run it as a separate session and keep the files apart
Gain / ISOYour scope's default or unity gain. Don't change it between sessions
Binning1×1
Minimum per session30 minutes of kept subs. Shorter sessions are fine but tell us
Session countAs many clear nights as you can manage. Ten sessions of 45 minutes beats one of 4 hours
MoonAvoid the three nights either side of full Moon for broadband. Dual-band is fine any time

Calibration frames

If your scope lets you take them:

  • Darks: 20 or more, same sub length, gain and sensor temperature as your lights, taken the same night or in the same week.
  • Flats: 20 or more, taken without moving the scope or changing focus. T-shirt or dawn-sky flats both work.
  • Bias: 50 or more, only if you aren't sending dark-flats.

Dwarf and Seestar users: your scope calibrates internally. Skip this bit.

What to send

Raw frames, every one

Send the raw sub-exposures as FITS, every one your scope kept. That is the only format that goes straight into the stack.

SendDon't send
Raw light frames, FITS (.fits / .fit)JPEGs or PNGs of any kind
Calibration frames, FITS, if you took themYour scope's live-stacked image (the pretty one the app shows you)
Raw DSLR files (.CR2, .CR3, .NEF, .ARW) if you shoot a cameraStacked or processed TIFFs
Frames you've already cropped, debayered or stretched
Screenshots

Why: a stacked image has already been aligned, averaged and usually stretched. It can't be weighted against other people's data and it throws away the per-frame information we need to reject satellites, clouds and bad tracking.

If you also want to share your own processed image, lovely, but send it separately and label it as such.

Folders and naming

One folder per session

Don't rename the FITS files themselves; the scope's own names and headers are fine. Just put each session in its own folder named like this:

YourName_Scope_YYYY-MM-DD_Filter

Examples:

  • AlistairB_ZWO2600MC_2026-11-14_Broadband
  • MarkS_SeestarS50_2026-11-14_DualBand
  • JoeT_DwarfIII_2026-11-20_Broadband

Inside that folder, if you have calibration frames, use sub-folders lights, darks, flats, bias. Smart-scope users just drop the FITS straight in.

One session folder per night per filter. Two nights = two folders. Broadband and dual-band on the same night = two folders.

Add a short notes.txt in the folder if anything was unusual: dew, high cloud, a wobbly tripod, a street light, a changed gain. We'd rather know than guess.

How to send it

Send as you go

Deadline for all data: 10 Jan 2027. Send sessions as you go rather than saving them up; it spreads the load and lets us flag a problem early.

  1. Choose the session folder in the upload box (link below), or drag it in. No need to zip it: the box sends the frames, any darks, flats and bias in their sub-folders, and your notes.txt. Don't paste frames into WhatsApp or email; they get recompressed.
  2. Drop a one-line message in the group when it's up: name, date, how many subs.

Upload link: together.oneoffx.co.uk/#upload

A typical Dwarf III session of 45 minutes at 15 s is about 180 files and 2 to 3 GB. If your upload is painfully slow, say so and we'll sort a drive or memory stick.

FAQ

Common questions

My scope is tiny / my sky is awful / I only got 20 minutes. Is it worth sending?
Yes. Weighting handles it. A short noisy session still adds signal; a session that never gets sent adds nothing.
I had thin cloud for part of the night.
Send it all. Frame rejection picks out the bad subs better than you can by eye, and the good ones are still useful.
Can I send a session I shot before November?
Yes, if it's raw FITS of the same target and you can say roughly when and with what.
Will my name be on the final image?
Every contributor is credited with their scope and total integration time.
I don't know what any of this means.
That's fine. Post in the group or message Mark and we'll walk through it. The only thing that can't be fixed afterwards is a night spent saving the wrong kind of file.
